Can we evolve a transformer instead of designing it?

Short answer: yes, and people do (neuroevolution / NAS / AutoML-Zero). The catch is one idea, and it's visual — so let's look at it.

The crux: reaching the bottom of a loss landscape.
Gradient descent walks downhill with its eyes open (it knows which way is down). Evolution is blindfolded — it can only hop randomly and keep the hops that happened to be lower.

Why the blindfold matters so much

On this toy problem there are only 2 knobs (x, y). Evolution's random hops still find the bottom — just slower. Now scale to a real network: not 2 knobs but a million weights. A random hop in a million-dimensional space almost never happens to point downhill, so evolution wastes ~99%+ of its trials. Gradient descent doesn't guess — the gradient hands it the downhill direction directly, in every dimension at once, for one cheap backward pass.

That's the whole verdict of the field, and it matches the split you were reaching for:

Kind of knobExample in a transformerBest toolWhy
Continuous (smooth, differentiable)the weight matrices — W_q, W_k, W_v, MLP weights Gradient descentsmooth ⇒ a gradient exists ⇒ eyes open. Millions of them, so blind search is hopeless.
Discrete (no gradient)# heads, # layers, block size, what connects to what Evolution / searchyou can't take a derivative of "4 heads vs 6 heads" ⇒ you must try them ⇒ that's a search ⇒ evolution is a search.

So the mature recipe isn't "gradients OR evolution." It's evolution on the outside picking the architecture, gradient descent on the inside training each candidate's weights. You had both halves — just route each knob to the tool that's cheap for it.

Your exact experiment, already run

AutoML-Zero (Google Brain, 2020) did the extreme version of your idea: throw out every tool — no "neural net," no "dot product," no "backprop" given. Hand evolution only raw math ops (add, multiply, mean…) and an empty program on tiny tasks. It rediscovered neural networks, gradient descent, and normalization on its own. That is "no intelligence, just evolve" — and it works.

The one dial that ties it together

Inductive bias = how much human structure you bake in vs. how much the machine must search for. Dial all the way to "search everything" → AutoML-Zero: beautiful, astronomically expensive. Dial toward "bake in good priors" → the transformer: humans injected a few strong ideas (attention, residuals) that shrink the search space so much that plain gradient descent finds great weights fast. The transformer won because it sits at a sweet spot on this dial — not because searching is wrong.
