From a cat's brain to the CNN

Convolutional nets are reverse-engineered from what Hubel & Wiesel found by sticking an electrode into a cat's visual cortex in 1959. Here's the whole chain — and the twist ending where the copy became the best model of the original.

1. The happy accident (1958–59)

They recorded from one neuron in the cat's primary visual cortex (V1) and flashed dots to find its trigger. Almost nothing. Then, sliding a glass slide into the projector, its moving edge swept the screen — and the neuron fired like a machine gun. The trigger was never dots. It was an oriented edge.

Each V1 neuron is tuned to one orientation; a whole population tiles every angle. That single fact — local, oriented edge detectors — is the seed of the entire convolutional idea.

6. The twist ending

In 2014, Yamins & DiCarlo trained a deep CNN only to classify objects — never told it a single fact about the brain. Then they checked its guts against real neurons: its middle layers predicted real V4 firing, and its top layer predicted real IT firing — better than any model neuroscientists had hand-built.

So the loop closed: a cat's cortex inspired the CNN, and decades later the CNN became our best working model of that same cortex. The copy explains the original.
