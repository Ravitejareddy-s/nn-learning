The shadow: which line are we measuring?

B is the ground. A is a leaning pole. The sun is directly overhead. Drag the top of the red pole and watch its shadow slide along the ground.

The green line is the shadow — that's the answer. The dotted line is just a ray of sunlight showing where the shadow ends. We read the green, never the dotted.

Read it in this order

1. Sunbeam (dotted)drops straight down from the pole's tip to the ground. Perpendicular = "straight-down light".
2. Shadow (green)runs along the ground from the base of the pole to where the sunbeam lands. This length is the measurement.
3. Dot product= length of B × length of the green shadow.
Why the dotted line has to be perpendicular: only straight-down light gives the pole's true reach along the ground. Tilt the light and the shadow would stretch or shrink dishonestly. Perpendicular = the fair measurement.
The sign lives in the shadow. Lean the pole toward B and the shadow falls on B's side (positive). Stand it straight up and there's no shadow (zero — this is what "perpendicular vectors have dot product 0" means). Lean it away and the shadow falls behind the origin, on the wrong side (negative).
