What am I actually looking at?

The dot product answers one question: how much do these two arrows point the same way? There are three equal ways to compute it, and the playground shows all three agreeing on the same number.

The algebra view (Step 1): line up the two vectors' components, multiply the matching ones, add. A·B = ax·bx + ay·by. That's it — no trig, just multiply-and-add. This is the version you'll write in code a thousand times.
The angle view (Step 2): the exact same number equals |A|·|B|·cosθ, where θ is the angle between the arrows. So the dot product secretly carries the angle. If you already know both lengths and the dot product, you can recover the angle — that's how similarity gets measured everywhere in ML (cosine similarity is literally this, with the lengths divided out).
The shadow view (Step 3): shine a light straight down onto B and look at the shadow A casts along B. The length of that shadow is |A|cosθ. Multiply it by |B| and you get the dot product again. This is why the sign matters:
angle < 90°shadow points the same way as Bdot > 0
angle = 90°no shadow at alldot = 0
angle > 90°shadow points opposite to Bdot < 0

Everything here is computed by the page from the two points you place — the grid coordinates feed straight into the formulas, so the numbers are exact, not illustrative.
