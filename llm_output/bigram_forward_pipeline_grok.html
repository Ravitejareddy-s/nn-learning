Bigram forward pass, spelled out with "ravi"

Dummy vocab of 6 chars. Watch the shapes change: word → indices → through the embedding layer → logits → softmax → probs → one loss number.

The whole pipeline at once

The orange box is the only layer with weights. Everything after it (softmax, -log, mean) is pure arithmetic with nothing to learn.

token / indices embedding layer (weights) logits probs targets

Step 1 — encode: chars become numbers

Every char is just its position in the vocab. "ravi" → [1, 4, 2, 5].

Step 2 — the embedding layer: a lookup table of rows

This is the matrix you asked about. It's V × V. Row i holds the raw next-char scores for token i. The indices just pluck out rows — no matrix multiply.

Feeding [1,4,2,5] grabs rows 1, 4, 2, 5 (the orange ones) and stacks them → that stack is the logits.

Step 3 — logits: the plucked rows (T × V)

Shape (4, 6): one row per input char, 6 raw scores each. Still just numbers — not probabilities yet.

Step 4 — softmax turns one row of logits into probabilities

exp() each score, then divide by their sum. Shown for the last row ('i'). This lives inside cross-entropy.

Step 5 — cross-entropy: grade each prediction against the true next char

For each position, take the probability at the true index (red box), do -log, then average all 4. That average is the loss.

loss = 2.554. Lower = the model put more probability on the right next char. Only the orange embedding weights get nudged to shrink this — softmax & -log have nothing to learn.
